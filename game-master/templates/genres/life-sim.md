# Rules: life sim

The ruleset for this game. It builds on the table rules in the project's
`CLAUDE.md` (checks, difficulty ladder, outcome tiers, Fortune, clocks,
conditions) and adds what a game about an ordinary life under pressure needs.
Reshape during session zero to fit the life being played; record later
changes under House rules in `game.md`.

The challenge here is the calendar. There are more people, ambitions and
obligations than hours, energy and money, and what is neglected does not
wait where it was left.

## Skills

Rated 0 to 5. A new character assigns 3, 2, 2, 1, 1, 0 to the six general
skills, then 3, 2, 1 to three specific skills of their own.

| Skill | Covers |
|---|---|
| Body | Fitness, stamina, sport, manual work, health |
| Mind | Study, analysis, learning something new, remembering |
| Charm | First impressions, small talk, flirting, persuading |
| Empathy | Reading people, listening, handling a hard conversation |
| Nerve | Acting under pressure, confrontation, taking a risk |
| Practical | Money sense, paperwork, fixing things, organising |

**Specific skills** are named by the player: a profession, a craft, a hobby
taken seriously ("Line cooking", "Backend engineering", "Violin"). A specific
skill is used in place of a general one when it applies.

## Resources

- **Money:** real currency and real sums. Track the balance, fixed monthly
  costs, and income. Rent is due when it is due.
- **Energy:** each day has three slots (morning, afternoon, evening). Work,
  study, people, chores and projects each take slots. A fourth activity in a
  day is possible and adds 1 Stress.
- **Stress** (0 to 6): rises with overwork, conflict, money trouble and bad
  outcomes; falls by 1 for a slot of real rest or time with someone close. At
  4 or more, disadvantage on Empathy and Mind. At 6 something gives: illness,
  a blow-up, a missed obligation. The GM picks, and Stress resets to 3.
- **Health and standing at work or school:** as conditions and clocks, not
  numbers, until they matter.

## People

Everyone who matters has a line in `index.md`, a `world/` entry, and:

- **Closeness** (0 to 5): 0 stranger, 1 acquaintance, 2 friendly, 3 friend,
  4 close, 5 family in the sense that counts.
- **Last real contact:** the in-game date.
- What they are going through themselves, in `secrets/` until the character
  learns it.

Closeness rises through time spent and things shared, usually one step per
significant shared event, and not on a schedule. It drops by 1 after a month
without real contact (two months at 4 or 5), and at once on a betrayal or a
badly broken promise. People notice who shows up.

People have their own lives. Each significant person has a clock or two of
their own (a job hunt, an illness in the family, a move, a relationship),
which advance in the world turn whether or not the character is paying
attention.

## Time: the week

The turn is one week. The calendar is real: weekdays, weekends, dates,
seasons, holidays.

1. **Look ahead.** The GM lists what is fixed this week (work hours, bills,
   appointments, things promised) and what has come up.
2. **Plan.** The player says how the free slots are spent, in broad strokes.
3. **Play.** The GM plays out the one to three moments of the week that are
   uncertain or that matter, as scenes, and summarises the rest in a few
   lines. Checks happen where the outcome is in doubt and something rides on
   it: the interview, the apology, the exam, the first date, the deadline.
4. **Event.** Roll 1d20 for the week. 1 to 2: a real setback (an injury, a
   layoff rumour, a big unexpected bill). 3 to 6: a complication in something
   already in motion. 7 to 15: an ordinary week. 16 to 19: an opening (an
   invitation, a lead, a chance meeting). 20: a turning point is offered.
5. **World turn.** Other people's clocks advance; consequences of earlier
   weeks arrive; neglected things decay.
6. **Accounts.** Money, Stress, Closeness and project clocks are updated,
   state is saved.

Slow down to a single day or a single conversation whenever that is where the
story is, and skip quiet weeks in a paragraph when the player wants to move.

## Long projects

Anything that takes months is a clock: learn the language, finish the
portfolio, save the deposit, get fit, write the book. Each slot spent on it
allows a check (difficulty by ambition); SUCCESS ticks once, CRITICAL twice,
PARTIAL once at the price of a Stress, FAILURE nothing, DISASTER a setback. A
project untouched for a month loses a tick. Finishing one changes the
character's life in a concrete way and is written into `state.md`.

## Losing

Nobody dies of a bad week, and nothing resets. The costs of failure are a
strained friendship, lost money, lost time, a closed door, a reputation at
work, Stress. Large losses are possible and permanent: the job, the
relationship, the flat. The game continues from wherever that leaves the
character.

## Advancement

A specific skill rises by 1 when its project clock fills. A general skill
rises by 1, to a maximum of 5, at the end of a chapter of the character's
life (a year, a move, a job, a relationship beginning or ending), when
Fortune is also restored to 2 if it is lower.
